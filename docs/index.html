How to read this

Every practice is graded twice. Evidence is scored against the specific outcome the community promises for it, never a weaker or kinder version. Risk is scored for how people actually do it. Each practice sits on the grid at its evidence score (left to right) and its risk (bottom to top).

Evidence graded against the claim

0 None
No evidence found, or the evidence contradicts the claim.
1 Anecdotal
Case reports, mechanism-only arguments, testimonials.
2 Limited
Observational or uncontrolled studies, small samples, or evidence only in a different population or indication.
3 Moderate
At least one RCT, or consistent controlled studies.
4 Strong
Meta-analysis or multiple RCTs in a comparable population, or regulatory approval for this exact indication.

Risk 0 to 4

Risk as practiced: how the community typically does it — self-administered, unregulated supply, no monitoring. This is the value that gets plotted.

Risk, supervised: done by a licensed clinician with a regulated product. Not rated if no legitimate supervised version exists.

0 Negligible
No meaningful harm expected.
1 Low
Minor, reversible, well-characterized side effects.
2 Moderate
Side effects that need monitoring, prescription-only, or a real chance of an unwanted permanent result.
3 High
Serious or irreversible harm is plausible, or the supply is unregulated with documented contamination or mislabeling.
4 Severe
Documented deaths or permanent disability at typical use.

Every practice, in detail

Face

Mewing

The claim: Holding the tongue flat against the roof of the mouth (correct "oral posture") reshapes adult facial bone, giving a sharper jawline, a more forward upper jaw and more defined cheekbones.

Evidence
None (0 of 4)
Risk as practiced
Low (1 of 4)
Risk, supervised
No legitimate supervised version exists
Verdict
–Low harm, low payoff

Why this evidence score

No trial or imaging study testing mewing on adult jaw shape turned up in a PubMed search, and the American Association of Orthodontists states there is no scientific evidence that it reshapes the jawline. Tongue posture matters for oral function, but that is a much weaker claim than changing adult bone.

Why this risk score

The AAO warns that chronic tongue pressure can loosen teeth, shift the bite and affect speech, but no study quantifies this. The bigger cost is time spent on a method that does not deliver while a real bite or jaw problem goes unassessed.

Key harms

  • Tooth movement or bite change from chronic pressure
  • Speech changes
  • Jaw muscle or TMJ tension
  • Delays a real orthodontic assessment

Safer alternative

An orthodontist consult for any bite, jaw-alignment or tongue-posture concern.

Sources

  1. Expert statement American Association of Orthodontists Warns Against Popular "Mewing" Trend — American Association of Orthodontists, 2024
  2. Expert statement Does Mewing Actually Reshape Your Jaw? — American Association of Orthodontists, 2025

Face

Bonesmashing

The claim: Striking the cheekbones and jaw with a hammer or similar tool creates microfractures that heal as denser, more projecting bone, producing a sharper jawline and higher cheekbones.

Evidence
None (0 of 4)
Risk as practiced
High (3 of 4)
Risk, supervised
No legitimate supervised version exists
Verdict
!Not worth the risk

Why this evidence score

No human study shows that repeated blows reshape adult facial bone; a plastic surgeon at Mater Private Hospital Brisbane says they cause swelling and microfractures that do not improve appearance. Even professionally treated cheekbone-arch fractures leave some asymmetry in 20-40% of patients, the opposite of the promised result.

Why this risk score

Deliberate blows to the face can fracture the cheekbone or jaw, and in a pooled analysis about half of patients with cheekbone-complex fractures had infraorbital nerve dysfunction, with numbness sometimes persisting long term. No documented deaths or case series of permanent disability were found, so this is rated High rather than Severe.

Key harms

  • Facial fracture needing surgery
  • Lasting numbness from nerve injury
  • Bite change and tooth damage
  • Permanent asymmetry or scarring

Safer alternative

A consult with an orthodontist or oral and maxillofacial surgeon, who can say what is realistic for your face and treat any genuine bite or jaw problem safely.

Sources

  1. Expert statement Mater doctors warn of dangers of bone-smashing trend — Mater (Australia), 2026
  2. Systematic review Evaluating infra-orbital nerve injury in zygomaticomaxillary complex fractures: a systematic review and meta-analysis — Frontiers in Oral Health, 2025
  3. Review Zygomatic Arch Fracture — StatPearls (NCBI Bookshelf), 2024

Face

Thumbpulling

The claim: Pulling outward on the roof of the mouth with the thumbs widens and advances the adult upper jaw, giving more tongue room, higher cheekbones and a better-balanced face.

Evidence
None (0 of 4)
Risk as practiced
Low (1 of 4)
Risk, supervised
No legitimate supervised version exists
Verdict
–Low harm, low payoff

Why this evidence score

A PubMed title and abstract search for thumb pulling returned no results. One studied non-surgical route to adult upper-jaw expansion is a miniscrew-anchored appliance, not finger pressure.

Why this risk score

No harm cases are documented, so this score is an inference from mechanism: uncontrolled finger force on teeth, gums and the jaw joint is most likely to cause irritation or soreness. The harms are not well characterized, which is why confidence is low.

Key harms

  • Gum and mouth-lining irritation
  • Tooth or bite change from uncontrolled force
  • Jaw joint strain
  • Delays a proper orthodontic assessment

Safer alternative

An orthodontist can assess palate width and bite with imaging and, if a narrow palate is confirmed, treat it with a specialist expansion appliance.

Sources

  1. Systematic review Efficacy of Miniscrew-Assisted Rapid Palatal Expansion (MARPE) in late adolescents and adults: a systematic review and meta-analysis — European Journal of Orthodontics, 2021
  2. Cross-sectional study Analysis of the Frequency and Correlated Factors of Midpalatal Suture Maturation Stages in Young Adults, Based on Cone Beam Computed Tomography Imaging — Journal of Clinical Medicine, 2022

Face

Jawline chewing gum / jaw exercisers

The claim: Chewing very hard gum or using resistance jaw trainers builds the jaw muscles and gives a sharper, more chiseled jawline.

Evidence
None (0 of 4)
Risk as practiced
Low (1 of 4)
Risk, supervised
No legitimate supervised version exists
Verdict
–Low harm, low payoff

Why this evidence score

A randomized trial in healthy adults (58 participants, six months of gum-chewing training) raised bite force but found no change in masseter thickness or jaw shape. A trial that did show masseter thickening tested chewing exercises in adults aged 65 and over, and one case report describes masseter hypertrophy as bulging at the angle of the jaw.

Why this risk score

A systematic review of 8 studies found gum chewing ranges from no link to jaw-disorder symptoms through to dose-dependent muscle discomfort and hypertrophy, which generally eases when chewing stops. No study of very hard gum or resistance jaw trainers was found, so their harms are unquantified.

Key harms

  • Jaw muscle pain
  • Temporomandibular disorder symptoms
  • Masseter hypertrophy (bulging at the angle of the jaw)

Sources

  3. Systematic review Association of temporomandibular disorders and other jaw anomalies in chewing gum users - a systematic review — Journal of Oral & Facial Pain and Headache, 2025
  4. Case report Benign masseter muscle hypertrophy — Brazilian Journal of Otorhinolaryngology, 2008

Facial exercises ("face yoga")

The claim: Daily facial exercises build the facial muscles, lifting the cheeks and tightening the jaw so the face looks more defined and younger.

Evidence
Limited (2 of 4)
Risk as practiced
Negligible (0 of 4)
Risk, supervised
Negligible (0 of 4)
Verdict
–Low harm, low payoff

Why this evidence score

One uncontrolled 20-week pilot of 16 completers (27 enrolled), all women aged 40-65, found small gains in cheek fullness and an apparent age about 2.7 years younger on blinded rating. A systematic review of nine earlier uncontrolled studies found the evidence insufficient; the pilot measured cheek fullness and apparent age, not jawline definition.

Why this risk score

The practice involves no product or procedure, so the score rests on mechanism: the cited pilot trial does not report on safety either way. The main cost is time spent on a method with limited proof.

Procedure

Dermal fillers (jaw/chin)

The claim: Injecting filler into the jaw and chin instantly gives a sharper, more defined jawline and a stronger profile without surgery.

Evidence
Strong (4 of 4)
Risk as practiced
Moderate (2 of 4)
Risk, supervised
Low (1 of 4)
Verdict
RxWorks — clinician first

Why this evidence score

The FDA has approved hyaluronic acid fillers for chin augmentation and, since 2022, one filler for jawline definition, and a systematic review found significant improvement in 17 of 24 chin studies, including four RCTs. Results fade, with global improvement ratings falling from 82-100% early on to 52-77% at 12-18 months.

Why this risk score

Bruising and swelling are common, but an accidental injection into a blood vessel can cause tissue death, vision loss or stroke, and the risk depends on the injector's anatomy knowledge and on whether the product is regulator-approved. In a review covering 2,259 treated chin patients, serious events were rare (one necrosis, one vascular occlusion) and lumps resolved once the filler was dissolved.

Key harms

  • Bruising and swelling
  • Lumps or nodules
  • Blocked blood vessel causing tissue death, vision loss or stroke (rare)
  • Permanent disfigurement with non-approved permanent fillers such as injectable silicone

Safer alternative

Use a licensed, medically trained injector with a regulator-approved, dissolvable hyaluronic acid filler, and avoid permanent fillers. If the chin or jaw is structurally weak, ask an orthodontist or maxillofacial surgeon whether a lasting fix suits you better.

Legal status

United States
FDA-regulated medical devices; approved fillers are indicated for chin augmentation, and one product is approved for jawline definition, in adults over 21; use in the nose, forehead and around the eyes is not approved.

Procedure

Fat-dissolving injections (deoxycholic acid, incl. Aqualyx-type products)

The claim: Injected 'fat dissolvers' permanently melt chin and jowl fat without surgery.

Evidence
Moderate (3 of 4)
Risk as practiced
High (3 of 4)
Risk, supervised
Moderate (2 of 4)
Verdict
RxWorks — clinician first

Why this evidence score

Randomized trials of the single approved deoxycholic acid drug show lasting improvement in under-chin fat, and a follow-up of patients who had responded to treatment found 82.4% still held at least a one-grade improvement at three years, against 65.0% on placebo. Controlled data cover only that licensed product under the chin; none was found for unapproved fat-dissolving products or for the jowls.

Why this risk score

The FDA reports permanent scars, serious infections, skin deformities and deep painful knots from unapproved fat-dissolving injections, and has logged 129 cases of adverse events, some serious, when the approved drug was used outside the chin, including vision problems and muscle or nerve injury. Its 2026 safety communication on the approved drug also counts 117 unresolved cases of lumps or masses, averaging about 5 months.

Key harms

  • Persistent lumps or masses
  • Nerve or muscle injury
  • Serious infection and permanent scarring
  • Vision problems when injected near the eyes

Safer alternative

Ask a licensed clinician about the regulator-approved prescription treatment for under-chin fat, which is approved for that area only, and start with supervised weight-management care if the fat is general rather than local. Avoid self-injection and any product bought online.

Legal status

United States
Only one deoxycholic acid injection is FDA-approved, for under-chin fat in adults; other fat-dissolving injections are unapproved.
Australia
A deoxycholic acid injection for under-chin fat in adults is listed as a prescription-only (Schedule 4) medicine.

Procedure

Lateral canthoplasty ('hunter eyes' surgery)

The claim: Surgically raising the outer corner of the eye creates a positive canthal tilt ('hunter eyes') that makes the face look more attractive and masculine.

Evidence
Anecdotal (1 of 4)
Risk as practiced
High (3 of 4)
Risk, supervised
Moderate (2 of 4)
Verdict
!Not worth the risk

Why this evidence score

Case series show lateral canthal surgery can reposition the outer eyelid corner, but mostly for lid-laxity indications in a mean-age-55 population rather than young men wanting a tilt. The one rating study found (an edited face, 2,081 raters) scored an elevated outer corner lower on attractiveness and masculinity, so nothing supports the cosmetic payoff.

Why this risk score

Even in expert hands, a 600-patient series reported 28 cases of temporary eye-surface swelling, one retrobulbar bleed (managed without surgery) and 18 repeat operations within two years, and over-tightening can round the corner of the eye. The authors call complications rare and minor, but no safety data were found for cosmetic work on healthy eyes outside specialist oculoplastic practice, and the change cannot be reversed.

Key harms

  • Eyelid malposition needing repeat surgery
  • Bleeding behind the eye (one case in a 600-patient series)
  • Scarring and a permanently altered eye shape
  • Temporary swelling of the eye surface

Safer alternative

Try non-surgical changes to the eye area first (sleep, sun protection, brow grooming, eyewear choice). If you still want surgery, see an oculoplastic surgeon (an eye surgeon) in your own country and ask frankly what is and isn't reversible.

Sources

  1. Case series Aesthetic Lateral Canthoplasty — Ophthalmic Plastic and Reconstructive Surgery, 2010
  2. Case series A Retrospective Review of Patients Undergoing Lateral Canthoplasty Techniques to Manage Existing or Potential Lower Eyelid Malposition: Identification of Seven Key Preoperative Findings — Plastic and Reconstructive Surgery, 2015
  3. Cross-sectional study Understanding the impact of eyebrow and lateral canthal height on the perception of character traits: A rating-based analysis of 2081 participants — Journal of Plastic, Reconstructive & Aesthetic Surgery, 2025

Procedure

Rhinoplasty

The claim: A well-done rhinoplasty permanently reshapes the nose to improve facial harmony and side profile.

Evidence
Limited (2 of 4)
Risk as practiced
Moderate (2 of 4)
Risk, supervised
Moderate (2 of 4)
Verdict
!Not worth the risk

Why this evidence score

Evidence is observational: a literature review reports 72-89% patient satisfaction, and a meta-analysis found facial cosmetic surgery produced no significant self-esteem change (g = 0.23, P = .268). Controlled evidence for the cosmetic result itself is thin.

Why this risk score

Revision rates run about 5-15% and the result is permanent, with a residual hump and tip irregularities the most common complaints. People with body dysmorphic disorder, anxiety or depression are more likely to be unhappy even when the surgery is technically sound, and male sex has been labeled a potential risk factor for dissatisfaction.

Key harms

  • Need for revision surgery (about 5-15%)
  • Persistent hump or tip irregularities
  • Dissatisfaction despite a technically good result
  • Excessive scarring

Safer alternative

Choose a surgeon on your country's specialist register who does a lot of nose surgery, ask to be screened for body dysmorphic disorder, get a second opinion, and reassess your goals before booking. Do not use filler as a shortcut, since the FDA lists the nose as an unapproved filler site.

Procedure

Hair transplant abroad (FUE, Turkey)

The claim: A cheap, all-inclusive FUE hair transplant in Turkey gives a dense, natural, permanent hairline for a fraction of the home-country price.

Evidence
Limited (2 of 4)
Risk as practiced
High (3 of 4)
Risk, supervised
Low (1 of 4)
Verdict
!Not worth the risk

Why this evidence score

FUE outcomes have been measured in uncontrolled series, such as a mean follicle survival of about 91% in 158 men in China and recipient-area density rising from about 6 to 37 follicular units per cm² at nine months in 52 men in India, and the 2017 European guideline found no randomised controlled trial comparing transplantation with no transplantation. Outcome data from high-volume package clinics in Turkey are scarce and not standardised (a 2025 narrative review of hair transplant tourism, with Turkey as its case study, calls this a "data black hole"), and a survey of ISHRS member surgeons found that, on average, about 5% of their patients came for repair of earlier surgery by another doctor or a black-market clinic (it does not mention Turkey), so the dense, permanent hairline promised for that setting is not backed by outcome data.

Why this risk score

The international society of hair restoration surgeons says clinics in Turkey that recruit foreign patients often rely on unlicensed technicians, with depleted donor areas, scarring, unnatural hairlines and infections among the problems its members report encountering. With qualified surgeons, published reviews put overall FUE complication rates at about 1.2-4.7%, mostly minor and self-limited.

Key harms

  • Over-harvested, permanently thinned donor area
  • Permanent pinpoint scarring
  • Unnatural hairline or poor growth
  • Infection or scalp tissue death (rare)

Safer alternative

See a dermatologist first about whether medical treatment should come before surgery, then book a hair-restoration surgeon licensed in your own country and confirm in writing that the surgeon personally makes the incisions and removes the grafts. If you do travel, verify credentials and arrange aftercare before you go.

Sources

  4. Case series Recipient Site Necrosis After Follicular Unit Excision Technique For Hair Transplantation: Evaluation of 18 Patients — Aesthetic Plastic Surgery, 2024

Procedure

Limb-lengthening surgery

The claim: Cosmetic limb-lengthening surgery safely adds several centimetres of permanent height.

Evidence
Limited (2 of 4)
Risk as practiced
High (3 of 4)
Risk, supervised
Moderate (2 of 4)
Verdict
!Not worth the risk

Why this evidence score

Two systematic reviews (795 and 760 patients) report average gains of about 6.7 cm and satisfaction of roughly 89-98%, but the first found all included studies were level IV case series or retrospective reviews with no RCTs. Height gain is well documented; the 'safely' part rests on uncontrolled specialist-centre data.

Why this risk score

Even in specialist-centre series, reviews report about 0.15 true complications per patient (new-bone deformity, ankle and subtalar joint stiffness), alongside frequent pin-site infection and ankle equinus, with some nerve injuries and fractures. The reviews do not capture cost-driven overseas clinics, so the as-practiced score is a judgment, not a measured rate.

Key harms

  • Infection, including at pin sites
  • Joint stiffness or deformity (ankle, subtalar)
  • Deformity or fracture of the newly formed bone
  • Nerve injury

Safer alternative

Non-surgical ways to look taller (posture work, tailoring, lifts in footwear) carry far less risk, and a GP or clinical psychologist can help if height distress is significant. If you still pursue surgery, use a specialist limb-reconstruction orthopaedic unit with published outcomes rather than a cosmetic clinic.

Sources

  1. Systematic review Cosmetic stature lengthening: systematic review of outcomes and complications — Bone & Joint Research, 2020
  2. Systematic review Aesthetic lower limb lengthening techniques: a systematic review of efficacy, complications, and patient satisfaction — Journal of Orthopaedic Surgery and Research, 2025

Hair

Topical minoxidil

The claim: Minoxidil applied to the scalp thickens thinning hair and regrows lost hair in male pattern baldness.

Evidence
Strong (4 of 4)
Risk as practiced
Low (1 of 4)
Risk, supervised
Low (1 of 4)
Verdict
✓Worth doing

Why this evidence score

It is sold without a prescription with FDA-labeled use to regrow hair on the top of the scalp in men, and meta-analyses of placebo-controlled trials find more hair growth than placebo. Only a subset of users get cosmetically meaningful regrowth, and gains last only while it is used.

Why this risk score

Common effects are scalp irritation and unwanted facial hair, and the label tells users to stop for chest pain, rapid heartbeat, dizziness or sudden swelling. One case report describes low blood pressure after far too much was applied, so overuse is the main avoidable problem.

Key harms

  • scalp irritation or redness
  • unwanted facial hair growth
  • chest pain, rapid heartbeat or dizziness (stop-use signs)
  • hair loss resumes if you stop

Legal status

United States
Non-prescription (OTC): FDA Drug Facts labeling covers regrowing hair on the top of the scalp in men.
Australia
Pharmacy medicine (Schedule 2), available without a prescription.
Canada
Listed as a non-prescription drug in Health Canada's Drug Product Database.

Hair

Low-dose oral minoxidil

The claim: A low-dose minoxidil tablet regrows hair across the scalp as well as or better than the topical solution, without having to apply a solution to the scalp.

Evidence
Moderate (3 of 4)
Risk as practiced
Moderate (2 of 4)
Risk, supervised
Low (1 of 4)
Verdict
RxWorks — clinician first

Why this evidence score

Four randomized trials (279 patients) found oral minoxidil no different from the topical on hair density or diameter over about 6 to 9 months, and the topical has placebo-controlled proof. The trials compared oral with topical rather than with placebo, the FDA does not list hair loss as an approved use, and nothing shows it beats the topical.

Why this risk score

The FDA label for minoxidil tablets opens with a warning about fluid around the heart and worsened angina in its approved hypertension use; in hair-loss use, reviews report mostly excess hair growth, swelling and dizziness, and fluid around the heart is extremely rare and often tied to compounding errors. It needs a prescriber who checks blood pressure, heart history and other medicines, which self-sourcing skips.

Key harms

  • unwanted face and body hair, about twice as often as with the topical
  • fluid retention and swelling
  • dizziness, low blood pressure or fast heartbeat
  • rare fluid around the heart

Safer alternative

Start with a licensed topical, or ask a dermatologist or GP about prescription options; if the oral route is on the table, they check blood pressure, heart history and other medicines first and follow up.

Legal status

United States

Why this evidence score

One lab experiment found sleep-deprived faces were rated less healthy and less attractive, a second found more visible fatigue cues such as darker eye circles and paler skin, and a comparison of 60 women links poor sleep quality with worse skin-ageing and skin-barrier recovery measures. The samples are small, the experiments test total sleep deprivation (31 hours awake) rather than 7 or more hours versus slightly less, and the visible effects were small.

Why this risk score

Getting enough sleep carries no meaningful harm. Ongoing trouble sleeping is a reason to see a doctor, not to self-medicate.
